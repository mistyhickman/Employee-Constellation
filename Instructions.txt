I want to design and eventually build an internal DocMe360 application that modernizes and replaces an existing Microsoft Whiteboard-based onboarding exercise.

BACKGROUND

During DocMe360 employee onboarding, employees currently use Microsoft Whiteboard to add sticky notes into several categories describing themselves and their professional background. The exercise captures useful organizational information, but the information is largely unstructured, difficult to maintain, difficult to search, and does not fully capitalize on the relationships that exist between employees, skills, industries, organizations, interests, and professional connections.

I want to rethink this as a modern internal application.

The initial use case is employee onboarding, but the larger vision is an internal organizational knowledge and relationship platform that helps DocMe360 understand:

- Who our people are
- What they know
- What they have done
- Where they have worked
- Which industries and domains they understand
- What they are interested in
- What they want to learn
- Who they know
- Who inside DocMe360 shares similar experience
- Where expertise exists within the company
- How people, skills, projects, clients, industries, technologies, and interests connect

Think of the long-term product as a combination of an employee expertise directory, skills inventory, relationship graph, organizational knowledge graph, onboarding experience, mentorship discovery tool, and internal networking platform.

The application should feel modern, polished, highly interactive, and distinctly more sophisticated than a traditional HR form or directory.

CORE PRODUCT EXPERIENCE

When a new employee first enters the application, they should be guided through an intuitive onboarding experience for building their professional profile.

Avoid presenting the experience as one enormous form.

Consider progressive disclosure, autocomplete, searchable selections, tags/chips, interactive cards, floating nodes/clouds, animations, and other modern interaction patterns.

As information is entered, visually represent it around the employee so that the employee can see their professional "constellation" developing.

For example:

MICROSOFT AUTHENTICATION AND AUTHORIZATION

DocMe360 already uses Microsoft's ecosystem for employee identity and access.

The application should NOT create a separate username/password authentication system.

Design Microsoft Entra ID integration as a foundational requirement.

Use:

- Microsoft Entra ID
- OpenID Connect / OAuth 2.0
- Microsoft-supported authentication libraries
- Authorization Code Flow with PKCE where appropriate
- Microsoft Single Sign-On

The desired experience is:

Employee opens application
        ->
Existing DocMe360 Microsoft identity recognized
        ->
Microsoft Entra authenticates employee
        ->
Application receives identity/token
        ->
Application determines authorization
        ->
Employee enters application

Where possible, users already authenticated to Microsoft 365 should experience seamless SSO.

Use stable Microsoft directory identifiers rather than relying solely on email addresses as application identity keys.

Explore Microsoft Graph for safely retrieving appropriate directory information such as:

- Name
- Email
- Job title
- Department
- Manager
- Profile photo

Do not unnecessarily ask users for information already available through the corporate directory.

AUTHORIZATION

Authentication and authorization must remain separate concepts.

Entra determines WHO the employee is.

The application determines WHAT they are allowed to do.

Consider application roles such as:

Employee
Manager
People Operations
Talent/Resource Manager
Administrator

Consider mapping Microsoft Entra groups to application roles rather than maintaining employee authorization manually.

Follow least-privilege principles.

Do not request broad Microsoft Graph permissions simply because they are convenient.

NETWORK / KNOWLEDGE GRAPH

The signature feature of the application should be an interactive organizational network.

Conceptually model information as nodes and relationships.

Example:

Person
 |
 +-- HAS_SKILL --> AWS
 |
 +-- WORKED_WITH --> VA
 |
 +-- INTERESTED_IN --> Artificial Intelligence
 |
 +-- WORKED_ON --> CDSP
 |
 +-- KNOWS --> Person B

Another employee might connect:

Person B
 |
 +-- HAS_SKILL --> AWS
 |
 +-- HAS_SKILL --> FHIR
 |
 +-- WORKED_ON --> CDSP

The application can therefore identify:

Person A <-- AWS --> Person B
Person A <-- CDSP --> Person B

Create an interactive visual graph/web/constellation where users can explore these relationships.

Clicking a node should expand relevant relationships rather than displaying the entire organizational graph simultaneously.

Examples:

Click "FHIR"
-> display employees with FHIR experience.

Click "VA"
-> display employees with VA experience.

Click an employee
-> display their skills, industries, projects, interests, and connections.

Click "Artificial Intelligence"
-> display employees, communities, projects, and related skills.

Allow graph filtering by relationship type, expertise, organization, industry, project, team, etc.

Team
Company
Authorized leadership/operations

Sensitive personal information should not be collected unnecessarily.

Do not infer sensitive personal characteristics.

Do not infer relationships from private communications.

Do not analyze email, Teams messages, meetings, or communication frequency to secretly determine who knows whom.

Explicit employee-provided connections are preferable.
